Title: OG09 Prescribing crackdown displacement
Points: 2
1.  Beginning in the early 2010s, several states tightened prescription opioid prescribing through prescription drug monitoring programs (PDMPs), pill mill laws, and clinician guidelines. Overdose deaths from prescription opioids leveled or declined in some states, but overall overdose mortality continued to rise sharply. From a systems perspective, which of the following best explains this pattern? Select all that apply.
[*] The intervention targeted one inflow (prescription supply) without addressing the underlying stock of people with opioid use disorder.
[*] People with established opioid dependence shifted to heroin and later illicit fentanyl, which were cheaper and more available.
[ ] The intervention failed because clinicians simply ignored the new rules.
[*] The intervention produced a substitution effect that the policy design did not anticipate.
[ ] Overdose mortality rose because PDMPs caused new cases of opioid use disorder.

Title: OG10 Treatment capacity vs. treatment access

[*] This structure tends to underinvest in prevention because funding is triggered by visible mortality rather than upstream risk.
