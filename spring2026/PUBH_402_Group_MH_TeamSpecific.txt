Title: MH14 Team map — element exclusions
Points: 2
1.  Your team's final system map explicitly noted that several elements were excluded from the analysis, with the team writing that they did not include elements such as racism, housing, or postpartum care because the assignment asked them to use the provided list. From a systems perspective, which of the following is the most accurate critique of this exclusion choice, given what the maternal mortality literature documents about U.S. outcomes? (Select all that apply.)
[*] The excluded elements (structural racism, housing, postpartum care) are well-documented as central drivers of U.S. maternal mortality disparities, so excluding them substantially limits what the system can explain.
[*] Module 4 explicitly permitted teams to add new elements with structural justification; the exclusion was a methodological choice rather than an assignment requirement.
[ ] The exclusions were correct because these factors are not part of the health care system.
[*] The map's ability to explain the persistence of racial disparities in maternal outcomes is structurally weakened by treating these elements as outside the system.
[ ] Boundary choices have no effect on what an analysis can explain.

Title: MH15 Team map — engagement loop
Points: 2
1.  Your team's final system map identified a reinforcing loop (R1) connecting Healthcare Engagement, Access to Prenatal Care, and Quality of Care: more engagement increases access to prenatal care, which improves quality of care, which further strengthens engagement. From a systems perspective, which is the most defensible critique of relying on this loop as a major leverage point for reducing maternal mortality disparities?
a)  Reinforcing loops cannot be used as leverage points.
*b) The loop assumes patients can act on engagement and that engagement translates into access — but for patients facing the structural barriers that drive disparities (limited Medicaid acceptance, OB deserts, provider bias, transportation constraints), the upstream conditions for engagement to produce access may not be in place, so interventions targeting engagement alone may produce limited results in the populations most affected.
c)  The loop is not a real systems concept.
d)  Engagement has no relationship to access or quality.

Title: MH16 Team map — provider bias placement
Points: 2
1.  Your team's final system map included Provider Bias as an element with two outgoing arrows: one reducing Quality of Care, and one reducing Healthcare Engagement. From a systems perspective, what is the most defensible critique of how Provider Bias is positioned in your team's map?
a)  Provider Bias should not appear in any system map.
*b) Provider Bias is treated as an isolated variable with effects on care, but the map does not connect Provider Bias to the structural conditions (training, institutional culture, accountability structures, segregation of patient populations across hospitals) that produce and reinforce it — so Provider Bias appears in the map as a fact about the system rather than as something the system produces and could change.
c)  Provider Bias does not affect quality of care.
d)  Provider Bias is fully addressed by current accountability systems.
